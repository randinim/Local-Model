In the context of Puttalam, centralizing this operational and production data is structurally impossible due to industrial secrecy.Machine Learning, Data Privacy, and the Federated SolutionlFederated Learning (FL) emerges as the optimal mathematical and structural solution to this pervasive data-silo problem. Originally pioneered by McMahan et al. [4] for privacy-preserving text prediction on mobile edge devices, FL permits distributed machine learning models to train locally on edge devices situated at individual data generation sites. Rather than transmitting raw operational data to a central repository, edge clients only share aggregated, high-dimensional mathematical weight updates with a central orchestration server. While researchers like Yang et al. [7] have successfully expanded FL applications into healthcare diagnostics to protect patient records and into smart manufacturing to protect proprietary machine telemetry, significant technological gaps remain regarding its deployment in highly volatile, physical environments. Most existing FL frameworks rely on synchronous communication protocols (such as persistent gRPC streams or direct REST APIs) and assume a stable, high-bandwidth enterprise IT infrastructure. In the context of remote, coastal salt pans located in developing nations, these synchronous protocols are prone to catastrophic failure due to unstable internet connectivity, high hardware thermal throttling, and corrosive operating environments.Industrial Digital Twins and the Centralized Data BottleneckIn addition to the problems we face in engineering, machine learning and Digital Twins are being used more and more in predicting the environment and making smart manufacturing decisions. There are two types: component heads and text heads.Component heads identify the different components of your paper and are not topically subordinate to each other. Examples include Acknowledgments and References and, for these, the correct style to use is “Heading 5”. Use “figure caption” for your Figure captions, and “table head” for your table title. For example, the paper title is the primary text head because all subsequent material relates and elaborates on this one topic. If there are two or more sub-topics, the next level head (uppercase Roman numerals) should be used and, conversely, if there are not at least two sub-topics, then no subheads should be introduced.
